Rajma Masala

Overview

  • Prep Time: 15 minutes (plus 8 hours soaking time)
  • Cook Time: 45 minutes
  • Difficulty: Easy
  • Servings: 4

Ingredients

  • 1.5 cups dried red kidney beans (rajma), soaked overnight
  • 4 cups water (for pressure cooking)
  • 2 tablespoons oil or ghee
  • 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1-inch cinnamon stick
  • 1 to 2 green chilies, finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes, pureed
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric powder
  • 1 teaspoon Kashmiri red chili powder
  • 1 tablespoon coriander powder
  • 1 teaspoon cumin powder
  • 1 teaspoon garam masala
  • 1 teaspoon crushed kasuri methi (dried fenugreek leaves)
  • Salt to taste
  • 3 tablespoons fresh coriander leaves, chopped

Procedure

  1. Pressure-cook the beans. Drain the soaked rajma and rinse well.Place the beans in a pressure cooker with 4 cups of fresh water, the bay leaf, cinnamon stick, and 1 teaspoon of salt.Cook for 5 to 6 whistles on medium-high flame until the beans mash easily between your fingers.Reserve the cooking liquid.
  2. Sauté the aromatics. Heat ghee or oil in a heavy-bottomed pan over medium heat. Add the cumin seeds and bay leaf, letting them sizzle for a few seconds. Add the finely chopped onions and sauté for 8 to 10 minutes until they turn deep golden brown.
  3. Cook the tomato masala. Pour in the tomato puree along with the turmeric, Kashmiri red chilli powder, coriander powder, roasted cumin powder, and salt. Cook on medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the mixture thickens and oil starts separating from the edges.
  4. Simmer the curry. Add the cooked rajma along with its reserved cooking water into the pan. Use the back of your ladle to lightly mash a small portion of the beans against the side of the pot to give the gravy a creamy body.Simmer uncovered on low heat for 12 to 15 minutes until the gravy reaches your desired consistency.
  5. Finish and serve. Stir in the garam masala, crushed kasuri methi, and heavy cream (if using). Simmer for 1 more minute, turn off the heat, and garnish with fresh coriander leaves. Serve hot with steamed basmati rice, jeera rice, or roti.
